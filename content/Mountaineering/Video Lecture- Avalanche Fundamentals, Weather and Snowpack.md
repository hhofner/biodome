**Ingredients for an avalanche**

- slope
- slab -> sitting on a weak layer
- trigger

## Weak Layers

- New snow
	- come on quickly
	- stabilize quickly
		- (hours-days, maybe weeks)
	- from storm/wind
- Persistent weak layer
	- depth hoar
	- surface hoar
	- near-surface facets

### New snow
Upside-down snowpacks: any snowstorm that starts with colder temperatures and then starts to warm up -> generally BAD, creates instabilities (unstable layer)

Right-side up storm: comes in warm, and leaves cold storm. More stable, better skiing and riding, fluffy snow on top and dense layer below

### Persistent weak layer
Weak Layers that form between storms = a much more persistent avalanche hazard

#### Depth Hoar
- forms deep in snowpack, generally makes up the base of snowpack
- lasts a long period of time
- dig snow pit, easy to see.
	- large sugary grains, cant make a snowball from scoop.

forms from: thin early snowpack, and cold temp
